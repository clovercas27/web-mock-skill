# Assessment and outreach

## Assessment format

Keep the main narrative useful for an agency preparing a pitch. Start with an executive assessment, preserve existing brand strengths, then use a finding table:

| Priority | Observation and status | Evidence | Action | Expected benefit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Choose priorities by impact on the user's likely task and confidence in the evidence. Describe expected benefits as a reason to test a change, not a measured outcome. Include a short scope and limitation statement, reviewed date, source ledger, and phased recommendations.

Examples of credible phrasing:

- "The supplied desktop screenshot places the product-selection section below a large story block. Bring selection guidance earlier so visitors can start shopping sooner."
- "The attached homepage HTML uses the company name alone for its title. Consider a descriptive title reflecting the offer. Other templates were not checked."
- "Performance has not been measured. Run a mobile field/lab assessment before attributing slowness to any script."
- "Add concise, factual answers about product fit and shipping so customers and search systems can understand the offering more easily. AI citation outcomes are not guaranteed."

Avoid "Your site is losing sales," "Your SEO is bad," "Your website is invisible to ChatGPT," or "This redesign will increase sales by 30%." Do not assign a fabricated audit grade just to make the proposal look quantitative.

SEO findings should distinguish recommendations from observed defects. A page does not need every structured-data type, a missing meta description is not a direct ranking diagnosis, and a canonical tag is a signal rather than proof of the indexed canonical. A screenshot cannot establish keyboard access, crawlability, loading speed, or search ranking.

Where policy or regulatory wording is inconsistent, identify the exact conflicting sources and recommend owner verification. Do not rewrite warranty, shipping, medical, pricing, or legal commitments from assumptions.

## Email approach

Write in the voice of a helpful service provider: specific, concise, respectful, and easy to reply to. Open with why this particular prospect is relevant. Prefer one clear opportunity and the concept you made over a list of every service.

An effective progression is:

1. Company-specific observation, neutrally phrased.
2. How the completed concept helps the visitor's task.
3. A concise mention of related SEO and AI-search content work if relevant.
4. One low-pressure CTA and editable signature.

Use plain language for GEO, such as "making your product information easier for search engines and AI tools to understand." Do not imply the brand has an AI visibility problem unless it was actually researched. Avoid empty praise, fear tactics, fake urgency, unsolicited technical jargon, and inflated business promises.

Subject examples are patterns, not a fixed set: "A homepage idea for [Brand]", "Making [task] easier on [Brand]'s site", or "A few website ideas for [Brand]". Adapt to actual findings.

The follow-up should add a useful detail or restate the small next step without pretending the first email was sent. No scheduled sending, contact scraping, Gmail drafts, or outbound messages are part of this skill.

If a mockup could not be created, disclose the incomplete deliverable and do not claim in the email that a finished concept exists. Do not add a demo link placeholder that looks like a real URL; keep send-preparation notes separate from the email body.
